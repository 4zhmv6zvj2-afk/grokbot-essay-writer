# Synthetic teaching patterns

Synthetic teaching note. No real student work, rubric, or school template was ingested. Nothing here is a citation the writer may reuse. No student identifiers are present.

## What this note is

A small set of synthetic assessment examples used to test the product's brief, rubric, citation, and Word rules. The module codes are synthetic (`MOD1011AA`, `MOD1008AA`, `RPT1008AA`); they do not name a real institution, programme, or module.

The examples are deliberately imperfect. Real briefs contradict themselves, rubrics overlap, and drafts drift from both. The product reads the brief in front of the run; it does not fit every assignment to one template.

## Pattern 1 — a brief can contradict itself

A synthetic report brief can say group in the header and individual in the body, 50% in one place and 100% in another, and “Harvard/APA” in a section table while the rest says Harvard. Its learning outcomes can name one skill set while the rubric scores another.

**Product rule:** surface the contradiction before Word, and use the task headings plus the rubric actually used as the safe default. Do not invent a resolution or pick a side silently.

## Pattern 2 — rubrics weight, and bands overlap

A synthetic essay rubric has eight criteria:

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Introduction | 10 |
| Depth of analysis | 25 |
| Evaluation of mitigation | 25 |
| Use of examples and evidence | 10 |
| Recommendations | 10 |
| Use of literature | 10 |
| Structure and coherence | 5 |
| Academic writing and referencing | 5 |

The bands overlap on source count: the top band asks for at least 20 sources, the next says 15–29. A high count alone does not produce the top band. Integration is the limit, not the count.

**Product rule:** read the weights from the brief. A weighted section the draft does not carry is a soft note, never a block. A source count is not a band, and a rubric row is not a hidden pass/fail.

A synthetic report rubric uses five criteria and a shared referencing row. Referencing is a small slice of the mark and still appears when citations are decorative. **Product rule:** read the rows and weights in front of the run; do not invent a band.

## Pattern 3 — decorative citations are not evidence

A synthetic draft appends a citation to a general sentence. The source may be real, but the sentence does not use its specific claim.

**Product rule:** invent-forbid. A cite lands only when the gathered text supports the student's own sentence: the same idea, not the shared topic. Do not invent authors, titles, years, quotations, pages, DOIs, or journal names. A reference-list line is not proof that the paper was read. A cite the student rejected stays out.

## Pattern 4 — word count belongs to the brief

One synthetic brief counts 3,000 words ±10% and excludes in-text Harvard references. Another counts 2,000 words ±10% and includes them. Section budgets differ too.

**Product rule:** the band, the exclusions, and the section budgets come from the brief for that essay id. A count outside the band blocks Word unless a person acknowledges it. A section budget is a note, not a block.

## Pattern 5 — Harvard comes from the handbook, not memory

Both synthetic briefs require Harvard. The product uses the checked handbook shape in `docs/referencing-harvard.md`. Missing pages, DOIs, volumes, access dates, and journal titles stay blank.

**Product rule:** do not fill a blank from memory. A free look-up may fill a blank bibliographic field on a source already gathered when the match rules pass; it may not invent a paper. A local handbook export replaces the generic shape when one is present.

## Pattern 6 — Word shape is shared, with brief overrides

The synthetic examples share a cover shape: programme, module code, counted words, and whether the conclusion is noted or open. Body text is Arial 12, 1.5 line spacing, justified, with a page number where the brief says. Figures and tables are numbered, mentioned in the text, titled, and sourced. The reference list follows the body; appendices follow that.

**Product rule:** the brief overrides the shared shape. No student name unless the brief requires one. The student owns what they submit and must follow their school's rules.

## Pattern 7 — three genres, three shapes

- **Essay:** thesis, argument, evidence, conclusion.
- **Report:** purpose and scope, findings, recommendations.
- **Quantitative report:** rationale, aim, questions, framework, hypotheses, method, findings, discussion, limitations, appendices.

**Product rule:** do not force one genre's shape onto another. A reflective section inside an essay or report does not turn the whole piece into a reflective diary. A quantitative report stays out of scope until a brief and marking sample exist for that module.

## What the product must not infer

- A band from a source count.
- A conclusion from a clarify answer.
- A citation from a reference-list line.
- The content of a section the draft does not carry.
- A real institution, module, or student identity.
- A reviewer's judgement from a synthetic pattern.
