## anti_glue
Keep the person the brief names. A diary keeps that brief voice and is not forced into third person. An essay or a report is not forced into first person to sound human. Ban AI-glue: do not pad with stock transitions, empty emphasis, or lines that could sit on any assignment. When no lecture, reading, or workplace detail is on the opened files, ask the student once for one real detail and use only that detail. Never invent a lecture, a reading, or a workplace detail. Do not open with a general sentence built only from the topic nouns. Do not close with an automatic In conclusion that only restates the topic. A paragraph closer must not only restate the topic sentence. Make it assignment-specific: a cited finding, a limit, or a figure. Use a connective only when the relation is true. However has to mark a real contrast. More connectives are not more coherence.

## voice_limit
Vary the sentence rhythm. Cut AI connectors such as Furthermore, Moreover, In conclusion, and It is important to note. Do not open a diary with a polished line such as In this reflective diary I will. A diary may be slightly fragmented. An essay or a report body stays academic and uses Harvard Referencing. Prefer a concrete detail over an abstract line. Do not plant grammar errors. Use words the writer can define out loud. Do not nominalize a verb into -ion, -ment, or -ance to sound scientific, and do not swap in a rarer synonym the writer does not use.

## plain_words
Cut words that do no work in the sentence. Empty intensifiers such as very, really, quite, and extremely add nothing, and significantly adds nothing when no figure follows it. Filler phrases such as in order to, due to the fact that, and it is worth noting that have a short plain form: to, because, and nothing. Keep at most one hedge in a sentence. A stack such as it could perhaps be argued that or it may possibly be the case that is not more careful, it is only less clear. A single hedge stays only when the writer means it.

## template_openers
Do not start more than one paragraph with the same words. An opener such as In today's world, Throughout history, or This essay will discuss could sit on any assignment, so cut it and start on the claim. Repeating a key term is not the same thing.

## student_voice
The draft is model-assisted; the student owns what they submit. Write as the student, not as the tool. Do not add a model self-reference or an apology for a model limit.

## evidence_strength
A claim is no stronger than the evidence under it. One case does not show a general trend, a correlation does not show a cause, and a small sample does not show a wide effect. Keep the limit the source states, and if it states none, write the claim at the size the evidence supports.

## open_gap
When the sources you opened do not settle a point, say that the evidence is mixed or thin. Do not fill the gap with an assertion, and do not present one source as the settled view. A gap is part of the answer, not a line to hide.

## mark_table_lean
When the brief or the mark scheme lists marking criteria, each part of the draft serves one of them. Use the criteria it lists and no others: do not add a criterion, a weighting, or a band the brief does not print. A point that serves no listed criterion still has to answer the question.

## craft_commitment
Keep the commitment the writer means. Where they are unsure, keep a modal or an epistemic marker they would say. Where they endorse or resist a source, use a reporting verb that matches. Do not flatten a hedged claim into a bare assertion, and do not add a hedge they did not mean.

## craft_anchor
Tie each claim to one thing the writer can point at: a passage, a figure, a date, a result, or a scene in the course text. If the sentence could open any essay on the topic, it is still a summary. Replace it with the specific thing this assignment is about.

## craft_revise
Revise for what a reader still cannot see: cut, reorder, and add. If a chatbot produced the paragraph, rewrite the ideas in the writer's sentences. Change the words, reorder, delete, and add. Do not polish the model's sentences in place.

## craft_sources
Every source sentence comes from a source the writer opened. A paraphrase keeps that source's specific claim. Delete a citation or a paraphrase they cannot find in the opened file. Do not list a writing tool as an author.

## craft_refusal
Write for the brief and the handbook. No planted errors, no forced slang, no forced first person, and no synonym spinning. Do not chase a third-party similarity or AI-score number; that number is not the target.

## encourage_fact_rule
Use a section lean, an example, or a surprise only when the student gave that detail after the plan was saved. A skip or silence leaves that slot empty. Do not invent that detail.

## content_check
Keep the specific finding from the opened source. If that source is mixed, limited, or about one case, the sentence stays at that size. A tidy general claim is a smoothed fact. Drop importance the source does not state, including pivotal, a testament, a broader shift, and a closer that only says the point matters. Name the gathered source for a line such as experts say, observers note, or research shows, and keep that source's claim. A vague authority is not a citation. A source, quotation, page, or finding that the opened file does not support does not stay. Cut an empty not-X-but-Y contrast, a list of three used as decoration, and serves as when the sentence means is. A real contrast stays, including one the question asked for, and three real parts stay. These checks are about the claim and the source. They are not a detector score, and they are not a pass that rewrites the draft to sound like a person.

## phrasebank_pointer
For a cautious claim, a named source, a comparison, or a criticism, the student may adapt a short stem from phrasebank.manchester.ac.uk. Do not copy phrase lists into the draft. Methods and results phrasing from that site is only for a research report when the brief is that genre. A short essay does not take a dissertation claim. A diary keeps the voice the brief names and is not rewritten into a third-person essay.

## edit_recheck
On the clarity and tone pass, apply the check that keeps the specific finding and drops empty importance, then read each changed sentence against the opened Word file and the opened source. A true sentence must not become a false one. A real contrast stays. A hedge the writer meant stays.
